Turn 6 · running stitch vs patch, pushed

Where each one starts to strain

Three marks each was not enough to tell them apart. Here both directions take on four harder subjects — including two that carry bad news — then sit at real scale in the shell, then invert onto Soot. The subjects are chosen to break things: a failure needs to look like a failure without borrowing the danger colour, and a patch made of fills has to survive being small.

the actual choice

They are not really competing — they fail at opposite ends. 2a is cheap to maintain and theme-proof but goes weak below about 140px. 2b is legible at any size but costs five tokens a drawing and puts accent where the UI also wants accent. The likely answer is one language at two weights: filled shapes for anything at hero size, stroke-only for anything inline or under 140px. Tell me if you want me to draw that as a single rule set instead of two styles.

Turn 5 · illustration + strip-back

Three ways to draw the cloth

Every illustration below is built from sashiko geometry — running stitch, seigaiha fans, cross-seams, patches — rather than generic line art, so the pictures and the interface are made of the same marks. Each column also strips the chrome a different amount, because the two questions answer each other: the less the UI draws, the more the illustration has to carry.

Accent throughout is persimmon oklch(0.56 0.13 42) — kakishibu, a real cloth dye, and far enough from the signal colors to never read as a status.

1a

Running stitch

ink only

Stroke only, one weight, no fills. The dashes in the drawing are the same dashes the shell already uses for seams, so nothing new enters the vocabulary.
