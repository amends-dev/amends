Decided on this screen
01Both names are shown, because both are real: the role is what you set, and the tier is what an incoming request or an offered tool is checked against. Hiding the tier would make the permission errors elsewhere unreadable.
02A role above your own is visible but unusable, with the reason attached. Hiding it would leave an admin wondering whether owner exists at all; showing it greyed says the rule out loud — you cannot grant past yourself.
03The last owner cannot be demoted or removed, and the refusal is stated before it is attempted rather than as an error afterwards. Promote somebody else first and the block lifts on its own.
04An invite carries its own four states, and they are not interchangeable. Expired and revoked look the same from a distance but mean different things: one lapsed, one was taken back, and only the second was a decision.
05The workspace mark is the first letter of its name rather than an upload, because the record has a name and nothing else. If you want a real logo it needs a field on Workspace and somewhere to keep the file — worth deciding before the screen promises it.
06How somebody joined is kept and shown — founded it, invited by a code, or added by hand. Restoring a removed person reuses their row, so a workspace never accumulates two of the same human.
